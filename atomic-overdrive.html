Overview

Race on any surface

Play as a Rollbot in a racing course consisting of 3 laps, which lies within the retro-futuristic city of Atomaville.

These competitive Rollbots use their magnetic spherical wheel to stick to surfaces and explore different paths regardless of the gravity. Jump between walls, drift along ceilings and roll along nearly any surface at high speeds.

My work

Level design & production

As level designer and project manager, my responsibilities included:

  • Created a track design pipeline
  • Designing, building and iterating on the race track & lobby
  • Planning, managing and supporting the team using HacknPlan
  • Working closely with artists, designing the environment and modeling assets to set-dress the world
  • Lighting and post-processing
  • Playtesting sessions, collecting feedback and balancing
  • Tuning the player stats, mechanics and catch-up mechanics
  • Working with a team of audio collaborators, implementing sound in FMOD
  • Composed 2 songs for the game

Track design

From SVG to spline

Track design

Designed, iterated, and balanced the race track from initial prototype to final version, continuously playtesting and incorporating player feedback throughout development.

Collaborated closely with the artist to set dress and finalize the visual presentation of the track.

Track creation pipeline

Designed tracks in 2D as SVG layouts, imported them into Unity, and converted them into spline paths to procedurally generate the final 3D track geometry.

Modular track pieces

Built to iterate fast

Developed a modular track creation pipeline driven by a spline system, enabling rapid iteration and scalable production.

Game audio

Sound lead & composer

Coordinated a team of 6 audio collaborators, facilitating communication and meetings to produce sound effects and music aligned with the game's artistic direction.

Integrated and iterated on audio systems using FMOD and code to ensure proper implementation in-game, and composed and produced 2 songs for the game: Away for the title screen and Runner for the lobby.

Learnings & experience

Wearing many hats

In a team of six, we all covered more than one discipline. Alongside level design and production, I helped out wherever the game needed it, and it taught me a lot about how each part of the pipeline affects the others.

VFX

Created visual effects to support gameplay feedback and the game's retro-futuristic look.

Music

Composed and produced Away and Runner, setting the mood for the title screen and the lobby.

Sound effects

Implemented SFX in FMOD and hooked them into gameplay through code, iterating until they felt right in-game.

Lighting & post-processing

Baked lights, set up post-processing volumes, and tuned screen effects for the game and the feeling of speed.

What I took away

  • Switching roles taught me the real cost of each request I make to art, audio and code.
  • Clear plans and regular check-ins keep a small team aligned, especially across disciplines.
  • Being able to cover gaps keeps a small project moving when time is short.
